# ANSWER KEY · STEP 1
## Article Recall & Learning Check
### *Why children become fussy eaters* — The Economist

> **Note:** Some questions are open-ended. The answers below are **model answers** based strictly on the article; students may use different wording if the meaning is accurate.

---

## PART A · KEY IDEAS

### 1. What is the article mainly about?

✅ **B. How children's eating habits and attitudes toward food have changed over time**

---

### 2. Complete the timeline

**Before the 20th century**

Children generally **ate what their parents put in front of them and had broad appetites**.

↓

**20th century**

Parents' attitudes toward children's food choices began to **change, with greater emphasis on children's autonomy and less support for forcing children to eat certain foods**.

↓

**Later**

Supermarkets, processed foods and advertising **increased the range of available foods and gave children a greater influence over what their parents bought**.

---

# PART B · RECALL THE AUTHOR'S IDEAS

### 3. Give TWO factors that the article presents as possible reasons for the rise of picky eating.

Possible answers:

**1.** The rise of **snacking**, which reduced children's hunger at mealtimes.

**2.** Changes in **parenting attitudes**, especially giving children more autonomy over food choices and avoiding forcing them to eat certain foods.

Other acceptable answer:

- The rise of supermarkets and processed foods.
- Advertising increasingly targeting children.
- Changing psychological ideas about children's food preferences.

These ideas are discussed across the article. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

### 4. What role did **snacking** play in the author's argument?

**Model answer:**

The author argues that the rise of snacking reduced children's hunger, which may have contributed to more finicky eating at mealtimes. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

**Key relationship:**

> **Rise of snacking → less hunger → more finicky eating**

---

### 5. Why did some parents begin giving children more autonomy over their food choices?

**Model answer:**

An influential study suggested that children made nutritious choices when they were left to make their own decisions. This research was later popularised in parenting books, encouraging parents to give children more autonomy over what and how much they ate. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

# PART C · THINK BEYOND THE TEXT

### 6. The article suggests that adults may have contributed to the rise of picky eating.

There is **no single correct answer**.

### Model answer — Agree

> **I agree because** the article suggests that changes in parenting attitudes gave children more control over their food choices and reduced adults' willingness to insist that children eat certain foods.

### Model answer — Disagree

> **I disagree because** children's eating behaviour may be influenced by factors other than parenting, such as individual preferences or the wider food environment.

⚠️ For this question, the important point is **giving a reason**, not choosing the same opinion as the author.
