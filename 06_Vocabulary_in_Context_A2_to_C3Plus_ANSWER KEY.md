### 32 · WORD FORMATION

> Children's food **preferences** can be influenced by advertising.

✅ **Answer: preferences**

---

### 33 · SYNONYM PRECISION

> The article discusses the **change** in children's eating habits.

This is grammatically correct.

However:

> **transformation**

suggests a more substantial or fundamental change.

Therefore, if the context describes a major shift:

> **The article discusses the transformation of children's eating habits.**

---

### 34 · REGISTER

#### a.

> **children**

is more formal/neutral than:

> kids

#### b.

> **a considerable number of**

is more formal than:

> a lot of

#### c.

> **improve**

is more concise and formal than:

> get better

---

### 35 · NOMINALISATION

> supermarkets expanded

→ **the expansion of supermarkets**

> parents became more concerned

→ **the growing concern among parents**

---

### 36 · COLLOCATION PRECISION

Possible answers:

> a **considerable increase**

> a **wide range**

> a **significant change**

> a **growing concern**

These are strong, natural combinations.

---

### 37 · SYNONYM RANGE

Original:

> Children changed their eating habits.

Possible alternatives:

> **Children altered their eating habits.**

> **Children modified their eating habits.**

> **Children adapted their eating habits.**

> **Children transformed their eating habits.**

### Nuance:

- **alter** → change, often slightly or in some aspect
- **modify** → make specific adjustments
- **adapt** → change to suit new conditions
- **transform** → change substantially

---

### 38 · ADVANCED WORD CHOICE

> The study had a major **influence** on parenting advice.

✅ Best answer in this context: **influence**

Why?

> **influence on**

is a very common lexical pattern.

However, **impact on** and **effect on** can also be grammatically possible in different contexts.

# 🟣 LEVEL C2
## ADVANCED COLLOCATION, IDIOMATICITY & LEXICAL CONTROL

### 41 · COLLOCATION STRENGTH

> ________ concern

Best answers depend on intended meaning.

Natural possibilities include:

> **serious concern**

> **deep concern**

> **considerable concern**

> **growing concern**

The important point is that:

> ❌ **big concern**

is possible in informal English but is less precise/formal.

---

### 42 · ADVANCED COLLOCATIONS

Possible answers:

> raise **concerns**

> pose a **threat / problem / challenge**

> exert **influence / pressure**

> have a profound **impact / effect / influence**

> play a significant **role**

---

### 43 · PHRASAL VERB vs FORMAL VERB

| Phrasal Verb | Formal Alternative |
|---|---|
| find out | **discover / determine** |
| deal with | **address / handle** |
| bring about | **cause / generate** |
| point out | **indicate / highlight** |
| carry out | **conduct / perform** |

---

### 44 · REGISTER TRANSFORMATION

Original:

> The researchers found out that children's habits had changed.

Formal:

> **The researchers discovered that children's habits had changed.**

More academic:

> **The researchers determined that children's habits had changed.**

---

### 45 · COLLOCATION ERROR DETECTION

Incorrect:

> The study **did a significant impact** on parenting advice.

Correct:

> **The study had a significant impact on parenting advice.**

Also possible:

> **The study had a significant influence on parenting advice.**

---

### 46 · NUANCE

> The new policy **considerably** changed the way people behave.

Best answer:

✅ **considerably**

### Difference:

> **slightly** = by a small amount

> **considerably** = by a substantial amount

Therefore:

> slightly changed

= small change

> considerably changed

= significant change

---

### 47 · IDIOMATIC USE

For:

> a habit that is very difficult to stop

Possible expressions:

> **a hard habit to break**

> **an ingrained habit**

> **a deeply rooted habit**

Best idiomatic answer:

> **a hard habit to break**

---

### 48 · COLLOCATION TRANSFORMATION

Original:

> The change had a big effect on my life.

Using **profound**:

> **The change had a profound effect on my life.**

More natural alternative:

> **The change had a profound impact on my life.**

---

### 49 · ADVANCED PERSONAL USE

Model:

> **I had to cut back on my social activities after taking on greater responsibilities at work, which had a profound impact on my daily routine.**

Possible components:

- phrasal verb → **cut back on**
- formal/advanced vocabulary → **responsibilities**
- collocation → **profound impact on**

---

### 50 · C2 LEXICAL CONTROL

Model answer:

> **Taking on greater responsibilities at work had a profound impact on my daily routine. Although I initially struggled to deal with the additional pressure, I eventually developed more effective time-management strategies. The experience also brought about a significant change in my priorities. In retrospect, it was a difficult but highly influential period of my life.**

## EXPERT LEXICAL CONTROL

Possible choices:

| Word | Strength / Nuance |
|---|---|
| changed | neutral |
| altered | modified |
| transformed | changed substantially |
| revolutionised | changed radically |

For a major but not revolutionary personal change:

> **The experience transformed my attitude towards learning.**

---

### 52 · COLLOCATION DESIGN

Possible answers:

> **profound consequences**

> **deeply concerned**

> **highly influential**

> **widely recognised**

---

### 53 · REGISTER SHIFT

Basic:

> **I found out that the course was much harder than I expected.**

Formal:

> **I discovered that the course was considerably more demanding than I had expected.**

Academic:

> **I came to realise that the course presented substantially greater demands than I had initially anticipated.**

---

### 54 · PHRASAL VERB → FORMAL → ACADEMIC

### Everyday

> **I found out that the system had changed.**

### Formal

> **I discovered that the system had changed.**

### Academic

> **I determined that the system had undergone significant changes.**

---

### 55 · NUANCE SCALE

Weakest → strongest:

> **change → alter → transform → revolutionise**

### Strongest example:

> **The invention revolutionised the way people communicate.**

This should only be used when the change is genuinely radical.

---

### 56 · COLLOCATION REPAIR

Incorrect:

> The experience made a profound influence on my career.

Correct:

> **The experience had a profound influence on my career.**

or:

> **The experience had a profound impact on my career.**

---

### 57 · LEXICAL DENSITY

Original:

> People are becoming more interested in learning English, and this interest is increasing every year.

Improved:

> **The growing interest in learning English is increasing every year.**

More natural academic version:

> **The growing interest in English-language learning is becoming increasingly evident.**

---

### 58 · ADVANCED PERSONAL VOCABULARY

Instead of:

> good / bad / big / small / change / thing

Model answer:

> **The experience had a profound impact on my priorities and significantly altered the way I approach my daily responsibilities.**

Notice:

- good → **positive / beneficial**
- bad → **detrimental / adverse**
- big → **substantial / significant**
- change → **alter / transform**
- thing → **factor / aspect / issue**

---

### 59 · MULTI-LAYERED LEXICAL USE

Model:

> **The growing reliance on digital resources has brought about a substantial shift in my study habits, although I still find it difficult to cut back on unnecessary online distractions.**

Contains:

☑ advanced collocation  
→ **substantial shift**

☑ phrasal verb  
→ **cut back on**

☑ nominalisation  
→ **reliance**

☑ precise vocabulary  
→ **distractions**

☑ formal register  
→ **brought about / substantial shift**

---

### 60 · C3+ MASTER VOCABULARY TASK

### Model answer:

> **The growing reliance on digital resources has profoundly altered the way I approach learning. Although I initially struggled to deal with the constant flow of information, I gradually developed strategies for filtering out irrelevant material. This shift has had a significant influence on both my study habits and my expectations of learning. In retrospect, what initially appeared to be an overwhelming change has become an invaluable part of my learning process.**

### Vocabulary features:

☑ precise vocabulary  
→ **profoundly altered**

☑ advanced collocation  
→ **significant influence**

☑ phrasal verb  
→ **deal with / filter out**

☑ nominalisation  
→ **reliance / expectations**

☑ formal register  
→ **invaluable / gradually developed**

# 🧠 VOCABULARY KNOWLEDGE CHECK

For the word:

> **influence**

### Word

> influence

### Meaning

> the power or effect that something has on someone or something

### Part of speech

> noun / verb

### Word family

> influence → influential → influentially

### Common collocations

> **have an influence on**

> **a significant influence**

> **a major influence**

> **exert influence**

### Verb pattern

> influence + object

Example:

> **Advertising can influence children's food choices.**

### Preposition

> influence **on**

> have an influence **on**

### Related expression

> **influenced by**

### Synonyms

> effect · impact · sway

### Antonyms

No single exact antonym exists in every context. Depending on context:

> independence · lack of influence

### Register

> neutral → formal/academic

### Example from the article/context

> **Advertising can influence children's food preferences.**
